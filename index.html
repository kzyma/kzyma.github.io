Day 1 Sept 4

Naples → Monopoli

Land and Head South

Touch down at Naples Capodichino at 1PM. Take the Alibus shuttle or a taxi to Napoli Centrale station (about 20 minutes, ~€5 or €23 fixed fare). From there, board the afternoon Marino Autolinee or Itabus direct coach to Monopoli — the bus departs from the Autostazione Metropark exit of the station, multiple departures through the afternoon, journey roughly 4 hours. You roll into Monopoli as the Adriatic light goes gold. Check in, walk to the harbor, find dinner. The trip has begun.

Bus: Marino or Itabus Book ahead — fills up ~4 hrs direct

Days 2–5 Sept 5–8

Monopoli, Puglia — 4 Nights

Adriatic Light & Old Town Lanes

Monopoli is the perfect entry point into Puglia — compact, beautiful, and not yet overrun. The medieval old town wraps around a working fishing harbor, with whitewashed alleys spilling into piazzas and rocky coves for swimming. Don't over-schedule this stretch. Spend mornings at the harbor with espresso, afternoons at the beach or on a rented bike along the coast, and evenings drifting through the old town. Make one day trip south to Polignano a Mare — 20 minutes by local train — for the dramatic cliff views and lunch above the Adriatic. Another day, head inland to Alberobello for the famous trulli landscape.

Day trip: Polignano a Mare Day trip: Alberobello Trulli Day trip: Castellana Grotte Harbor dinners Coastal cycling

Day 6 Sept 9

Monopoli → Lecce

South to the Baroque Capital

Take the Trenitalia Regionale from Monopoli station, heading south along the Adriatic coastal line. About 1.5 hours, departures every 1–2 hours. Arrive mid-morning in Lecce. Walk directly from the station into the old town — within five minutes you're standing in one of the most extraordinary baroque cityscapes in Europe. Drop your bags, then just start walking. Save Piazza del Duomo for late afternoon when the light turns the golden Lecce stone warm amber.

Train ~1.5 hrs Trenitalia Regionale Walk from station

Days 7–8 Sept 10–11

Lecce, Salento — 3 Nights

The Florence of the South

Lecce is genuinely one of Italy's great surprises — a complete baroque city built in warm golden sandstone, where every church façade is carved with angels, vines, and garlands in exquisite detail. Spend two days here. Walk the Basilica di Santa Croce, Piazza Sant'Oronzo with its Roman amphitheater, and the winding Viale dei Filosofi. Try a pasticciotto (the local custard pastry, get it from Natale or Caffè Alvino) for breakfast every morning. Salento's wines — Primitivo and Negroamaro — are exceptional and cheap. Take an afternoon trip to Otranto for its Byzantine mosaics and clifftop Aragonese castle, or south toward the tip of the peninsula at Santa Maria di Leuca.

Basilica di Santa Croce Piazza del Duomo at dusk Day trip: Otranto Day trip: Gallipoli Pasticciotto for breakfast Negroamaro wine

Day 9 Sept 12

Lecce → Ostuni (Masseria)

Into the Olive Groves

Pick up a rental car in Lecce this morning — Europcar and Hertz have locations near the train station, book in advance. Drive north on the SS16 coastal road or cut inland through the Valle d'Itria. The drive is about 1.5 hours. Stop in Ostuni itself — park at the base of the hill and walk up into the old town for an hour. The "White City" is everything the name promises: chalk-white buildings stacked up a hilltop, visible for miles across the olive plain below. Then head 5km south of town to Masseria Il Frantoio on the SS16. Check in, breathe. Have dinner on the property tonight.

Rent car in Lecce Stop: Ostuni old town ~1.5 hr drive Dinner at Frantoio

Days 10–11 Sept 13–14

Masseria Il Frantoio, Ostuni — 3 Nights

Slowness Among the Olives

This is the decompression stretch — the whole point of a masseria. Masseria Il Frantoio is a working 72-hectare farm surrounded by centuries-old olive trees, producing four types of organic DOP olive oil. The 16 rooms are in 16th- and 19th-century farm buildings: terracotta floors, vaulted limestone ceilings, stone fireplaces, wrought-iron beds. Owner Armando leads a property tour before dinner each evening, narrating the food and its origins. Breakfasts are a full regional spread. The pool sits among ancient olive trees. Spend at least one day simply not leaving — pool, book, aperitivo at sunset. Use the car for a day in the Valle d'Itria: Locorotondo, Cisternino, or Martina Franca are all within 30 minutes and beautiful. Drop the car at Ostuni station (4km away) before your Naples train on Day 12.

Armando's nightly dinner tour Pool among olive trees Organic olive oil tasting Day trip: Locorotondo Day trip: Cisternino Breakfast included

Day 12 Sept 15

Ostuni → Naples

Return to the Volcano's City

Return the rental car at Ostuni station (4km from the masseria — arrange a taxi with the hotel). From Ostuni, take a regional train north to Bari Centrale, then connect to a Frecciargento or Intercity toward Naples Centrale. Total journey is around 4.5–5 hours. Book the Bari–Naples leg on Trenitalia.com in advance. Arrive into Naples early evening — drop your bags at the hotel in Chiaia or the centro storico and head straight out for dinner. You have two nights left.

Day 13 Sept 15–16

Naples, Campania — 2 Nights

Naples, Unfiltered

Naples doesn't need an agenda — it needs surrender. Walk the Spaccanapoli axis, the straight line that bisects the ancient city. Visit the Museo Nazionale Archeologico for the world's greatest collection of Roman artifacts from Pompeii and Herculaneum (including the Farnese collection and the Secret Cabinet). Take the Circumvesuviana train out to Pompeii for the morning, or Herculaneum if you want fewer crowds and better preservation. For food: sfogliatella from Attanasio near the station for breakfast, pizza at Sorbillo on Via dei Tribunali for dinner. Cocktails and nightlife in the Chiaia district — that's where Naples hides its good bars.

Museo Nazionale Archeologico Day trip: Pompeii or Herculaneum Spaccanapoli walk Pizza: Sorbillo Sfogliatella: Attanasio Nightlife: Chiaia

Where to Stay

Four Addresses
Worth Booking Now

Stop 1 · Monopoli · 4 Nights

Hotel Don Ferrante

Former Sea Fortress · 10 Rooms · Dimore di Charme

A converted clifftop fortress with just ten rooms, each featuring vaulted stone ceilings and direct Adriatic views. The rooftop hydromassage pool and terrace dining are exceptional. The Locanda Don Ferrante restaurant downstairs is one of the best in Monopoli. Only 10 rooms — book first and fast.

Sept 4 – Sept 8

Stop 1 · Alternative · Monopoli

Il Melograno

16th-Century Masseria · Countryside · Puglia's Original Luxury Farm

Puglia's first masseria-to-hotel conversion: a 16th-century farmhouse 5km from Monopoli surrounded by olive trees, with Moorish courtyards, spa, and a private beach club shuttle. The classic countryside option if you prefer fields over the old town.

Sept 4 – Sept 8

Stop 2 · Lecce · 3 Nights

La Fiermontina

Aristocratic Palazzo · Historic Center · Design & Art

A converted aristocratic palazzo in Lecce's old town, rated among Italy's finest boutique hotels. Contemporary sculpture garden, beautiful pool, and rooms that feel like staying inside a gallery set in a 17th-century shell. The bar is excellent and everything is walkable.

Sept 8 – Sept 11

Stop 2 · Alternative · Lecce

Risorgimento Resort

5-Star · Old Town · Rooftop Restaurant

Lecce's most established luxury property — a fully restored historic building in the centro storico with rooftop bar, spa, and a strong breakfast. More resort-scaled than La Fiermontina and easier to book. Right in the middle of everything.

Sept 8 – Sept 11

⭑ Stop 3 · Ostuni · 3 Nights — The Highlight

Masseria Il Frantoio

Working Farm · 16th & 19th Century · 72 Hectares · Slow Living

A working 72-hectare organic olive farm 5km south of Ostuni, in farm buildings from the 16th and 19th centuries. Sixteen rooms with terracotta floors, limestone vaulted ceilings, and stone fireplaces. Owner Armando personally leads a property and farm tour before dinner each evening. Pool set among ancient olive trees. Breakfast included — a full regional spread. Jasmine-scented courtyard. Deeply, authentically Pugliese. This is the one to savour.

Sept 11 – Sept 14

Stop 4 · Naples · 2 Nights

Palazzo Alabardieri

Restored Palazzo · Chiaia District · Boutique Luxury

A lovingly restored historic palazzo in Naples' elegant Chiaia neighborhood — quieter and more refined than the centro storico but walking distance to everything. Rooftop breakfast terrace, classic Neapolitan décor, excellent service. The nightlife and restaurants of Chiaia are right outside the door for your final two evenings.

Sept 14 – Sept 16

Trenitalia Regionale from Monopoli, heading south on the coastal line. Departures every 1–2 hours. About 1.5 hours direct. Tickets ~€12–18 on Trenitalia.com. Arrive Lecce Centrale — old town is a short walk.

Departure Day

Naples → Capodichino Airport

Capodichino (NAP) is 7km from central Naples. Official taxis have a fixed rate from the city center (~€23). Alibus shuttle from Piazza Garibaldi: €5, ~15 min. Book the taxi the night before and leave plenty of time — Naples traffic.

Practical Notes

Things Worth
Knowing

🌡️

September Weather

Early September in the deep south is still very warm — 28–32°C with some humidity. It softens mid-month. The Adriatic is perfect for swimming. Pack light: linen, breathable fabrics. Evenings can be mild but bring one light layer for the masseria.

🍽️

Eating in Puglia

The food is earthy and ingredient-led: orecchiette with broccoli rabe (cime di rapa), burrata made that morning, grilled octopus, fava bean purée with chicory, taralli. In Lecce: pasticciotto pastry for breakfast, every day. Reservations at dinner recommended for September.

🏨

What to Book First

Don Ferrante has only 10 rooms — book the moment you have flights confirmed. Masseria Il Frantoio has 16, also fills fast in September. La Fiermontina is small too. Book all hotels directly if possible — better rates and the properties appreciate it.

🚌

Transport Booking

Book the Naples–Monopoli coach at least a week ahead — afternoon departures fill up. Trenitalia intercity connections (Bari → Naples) should be reserved in advance. The rental car just needs a Lecce pickup confirmed; drop at Ostuni station.

🫒

At the Masseria

Don't skip Armando's pre-dinner tour — it's the signature experience. Arrive on the first evening with no plan. Ask about the olive oil pressing schedule (harvest often starts in October but early varieties can begin in September). The jasmine courtyard at aperitivo hour is not to be missed.

🍕

Naples Essentials

Sfogliatella from Attanasio (near Centrale) for breakfast. Pizza from Sorbillo on Via dei Tribunali for dinner — arrive at opening or queue. Cocktails in Chiaia afterward. For Pompeii, the Circumvesuviana train from Piazza Garibaldi takes about 40 minutes to Pompei Scavi station.
